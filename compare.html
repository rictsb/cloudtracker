The gating factors

balance sheets, one megawatt
Three ways to reach the same megawatt. IREN buys the land and substation and waits for power; CoreWeave rents a finished hall and pays for it monthly for 7–16 years; Nebius does both. Everything downstream — silicon, contracts, customers, debt — is bought in the same market from the same vendors and lenders.sections 1–2 of each page
Where they already meet. Investment-grade-backed GPU debt prices at ~6% for all three, non-IG at ~9%, prepayments have become standard on new deals, silicon costs the same per MW, and new capacity is signing on 3–5 year take-or-pay at $20m+ per MW whoever the landlord is.rows 4, 7, 9–11, 15
Where they will not. The shell is either $15m of capex or $1.5m+ a year of rent; power is either $35/MWh at your own substation or a market price passed through; and the customer book is either two hyperscalers or a broader tail. Those are choices, not stages.rows 1–3, 8, 12

Read the waterfall per megawatt, not in total. CoreWeave’s 2030 EBITDA is larger because it runs 7 GW to IREN’s 3 GW, not because a rented megawatt earns more. Per megawatt-year IREN earns about $14.6m of EBITDA and keeps $6.6m; CoreWeave earns $8.4m and keeps $2.1m; Nebius earns $11.0m and keeps $4.3m.chart above · page §5 of each
Rent hides inside EBITDA; the shell hides inside capex. A tenant’s building cost is a running cost, so its margin is lower but its capex per MW is $33m instead of $49m. The like-for-like test is cash kept on the capital the company itself deploys: IREN 13%, Nebius 12%, CoreWeave 6%.table above
Renting is faster, not richer. The landlord’s capital plus 20% prepayments plus 90% GPU financing let CoreWeave add more than twice IREN’s megawatts with less equity per MW. The price is rent for 7–16 years whether or not the racks are full, 9–9.75% senior notes, and half the revenue per MW on a hyperscaler-anchored book.page §4 of each

Scale is the tenant’s answer. On the pages as they stand, CoreWeave reaches $103bn of run-rate on 7 GW, Nebius $84bn on 4.65 GW, IREN $62bn on 3 GW. CoreWeave gets there with the most debt and the most prepayments still owed; IREN with the most equity per megawatt.table above
The multiple does the rest. $1 of run-rate is worth 4.06× to IREN, 3.34× to Nebius, 2.75× to CoreWeave, so IREN’s smaller book is worth more per share of ARR. Whether density pricing continues after 2030, and whether the refresh cost rises with it, is the open method question on all three pages.page §5–6 of each
Against the market. IREN trades at a fraction of its base; CoreWeave and Nebius trade near or above theirs. Either the market prices IREN’s unsigned 87% of 2030 revenue at close to zero, or it prices the tenants’ scale at more than the rented megawatt keeps.bars above

Converging or structural

What is converging (evidence to date). The cost of GPU debt by credit tier (6% IG-backed, 9% non-IG at all three by mid-2026); the silicon bill per MW; prepayment as a standard term; contract length on new capacity (3–5 years); and price per MW-yr by tenor, where IREN’s 3-year enterprise deals, Nebius’s mid-term deals and CoreWeave’s new Blackwell/Rubin pricing sit in the same $20m+ band. A convergence research round is verifying each of these with dated prints and will replace this note.research in progress, 8 Sep 2026
What is structural. Owning versus renting the shell, the power price that comes with it, the GPU book life each company has chosen, and the customer concentration each started with. These set the margin wedge (65% vs 57%) and the capital per MW ($49m vs $33m), and they do not close with time; they are the strategy.rows marked structural
How the models treat it. From 2027 the three pages already price new capacity from one market: the same spot rate, the same silicon-cost curve, the same lender advance rates and the same steady-state method. What stays company-specific is the signed book each carries into 2027 and the three structural rows. CoreWeave’s legacy book now has a re-contracting mechanism available for when its 2023–25 terms end.ramp-core renew · playbook §6e
