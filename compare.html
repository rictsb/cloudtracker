The gating factors

balance sheets, one megawatt
Three ways to reach the same megawatt. IREN buys the land and substation and waits for power; CoreWeave rents a finished hall and pays for it monthly for 7–16 years; Nebius does both. Everything downstream — silicon, contracts, customers, debt — is bought in the same market from the same vendors and lenders.sections 1–2 of each page
Where they already meet. Investment-grade-backed GPU debt prices at ~6% for all three and non-IG at ~9%; silicon costs the same per MW and escalates alike; book lives sit at 5–6 years; take-or-pay is the form and prepayment size follows the counterparty; new capacity signs on 3–5 year terms at $20–25m per MW for 3-year paper whoever the landlord is; each anchors on a hyperscaler and then diversifies; and all three ship Rubin in 2027.rows 4–11, 15, 17
Where they will not. The shell is either $15m of capex or ~$2m a year of rent; power is either $30/MWh at your own substation or a market price passed through; corporate debt is either 0–4.5% converts or 9–9.75% bonds until refinanced; the dilution path and the NVIDIA cohort are set by each history. Those are choices and vintages, not stages — but each has a date on which it moves.rows 1–3, 12–14

Read the waterfall per megawatt, not in total. CoreWeave’s 2030 EBITDA is larger because it runs 7 GW to IREN’s 3 GW, not because a rented megawatt earns more. Per megawatt-year IREN earns about $14.6m of EBITDA and keeps $6.6m; CoreWeave earns $8.4m and keeps $2.1m; Nebius earns $11.0m and keeps $4.3m.chart above · page §5 of each
Rent hides inside EBITDA; the shell hides inside capex. A tenant’s building cost is a running cost, so its margin is lower but its capex per MW is $33m instead of $49m. The like-for-like test is cash kept on the capital the company itself deploys: IREN 13%, Nebius 11%, CoreWeave 6%.table above
Renting is faster, not richer. The landlord’s capital plus 20% prepayments plus 90% GPU financing let CoreWeave add more than twice IREN’s megawatts with less equity per MW. The price is rent for 7–16 years whether or not the racks are full, 9–9.75% senior notes, and half the revenue per MW on a hyperscaler-anchored book.page §4 of each
Most of the per-MW gap is tenor and vintage, not the megawatt. All three count the same megawatt (the power reaching the computers), so CoreWeave’s $14m and IREN’s $22.5m are like for like on the megawatt. Hold IREN’s 65% margin and put it on CoreWeave’s $14m: it keeps $2.0m, the same as CoreWeave. The rest of IREN’s $6.6m is the 3-year enterprise price ($22.5m) assumed on the 87% of its 2030 book that is unsigned; its own signed 5-year Microsoft print ($9.7m) keeps about nothing after a four-year refresh. CoreWeave’s side is 98% five-year take-or-pay, the cheapest tenor, with 1.5 GW of 2025–26 paper at ~$10m locked to 2030–31. The like-for-like row (costs held in dollars) flips the order, which says the running-cost lines are not yet comparable: IREN’s 65% implies $7.9m per MW of costs against a filed 87% gross margin.rows above · CoreWeave pricing research 8 Sep and parity research 10 Sep 2026

Scale is the tenant’s answer. On the pages as they stand, CoreWeave reaches $93bn of run-rate on 7 GW, Nebius $84bn on 4.65 GW, IREN $62bn on 3 GW. CoreWeave gets there with the most debt and the most prepayments still owed; IREN with the most equity per megawatt.table above
The multiple does the rest. $1 of run-rate is worth 4.06× to IREN, 3.34× to Nebius, 2.75× to CoreWeave, so IREN’s smaller book is worth more per share of ARR. Whether density pricing continues after 2030, and whether the refresh cost rises with it, is the open method question on all three pages.page §5–6 of each
Against the market. IREN trades at a fraction of its base; CoreWeave and Nebius trade near or above theirs. Either the market prices IREN’s unsigned 87% of 2030 revenue at close to zero, or it prices the tenants’ scale at more than the rented megawatt keeps.bars above

Converging or structural

What converges (dated prints). Price per IT MW-yr at a given tenor: 3-year $20–25m at all three (IREN above $20m and discussions ~$25m, 27 Aug 2026; Nebius $20–25m, 12 Aug 2026; CoreWeave’s committed floor ~$20m, 8 Sep 2026), 5-year GB300 at $13–14m per IT MW (Nebius–Microsoft $13.1–13.9m on >100k GB300; SemiAnalysis GB300 6-yr $4.00 × 400 = $14m; IREN states ~$19m, unprinted), short paper at ~2×. The megawatt itself is already common: all three report critical-IT MW (the earlier 20% haircut on CoreWeave was wrong and has been removed). Contract length from both sides (IREN 2 → 3–5 years; Nebius months → 1–3 years core; CoreWeave ~5 years plus a 2–3 year sleeve). Take-or-pay as the form, prepayments by counterparty (hyperscalers 20–40% of contract value, AI-natives 45–60% of capex). IG-backed GPU debt at SOFR+225–250 within 25 bp; non-IG at SOFR+450–550. Silicon per MW and its escalation; 5–6 year book lives; ~2-year paybacks; a hyperscaler anchor that diversifies. The round’s verdict: a 2027–30 model should carry one market for revenue, silicon and GPU financing, not three.three-way convergence research, five strands adversarially verified, 8 Sep 2026 · ~/Research/convergence-2026-09-08.json
What is structural, and when it moves. The layer under the GPU: housing and power at ~$1.0–1.3m per IT MW-yr owned (IREN), ~$0.6–1.0m owned and ~$2.5–3m at build-to-suit sites (Nebius), ~$1.9–2.9m leased (CoreWeave) — 5–10% of revenue at $20–25m and second-order to silicon; the EBITDA margin that follows (85% project at IREN, ~60% CoreWeave, 50% and rising Nebius); power price and grid exposure by site; the form of the shell obligation (unencumbered freehold vs $52bn of lease commitments vs a $13.6bn lease book); CoreWeave’s 9–9.75% corporate layer until the 2030–32 notes refinance; non-IG debt tenor and recourse; the dilution path; NVIDIA cohort timing; and the demand backstops only CoreWeave (NVIDIA $6.3bn) and Nebius (Meta $15bn) hold. Each has a date: CoreWeave self-builds from H2-26, Nebius’s owned share rises with each campus, CoreWeave refinances from 2030.same round, structural list
How the pages treat it. From 2027 the three pages already price new capacity from one market: the same spot rate, silicon curve, lender advance rates and steady-state method. The round adds that IREN’s Microsoft print ($9.7m) and CoreWeave’s 2023–24 private-credit facilities are vintage artefacts to roll off, not to extrapolate; that IREN’s 65% is an assumption where the tenants’ margins are filed; and that the per-MW gap on this page is tenor mix (CoreWeave ~90/10 five-year/short vs ~60/40 at IREN and ~50/50 at Nebius, worth 20–25% at full price parity) plus CoreWeave’s locked 2025–26 book — new-contract prices at equal tenor and generation are at parity within a ±15–30% band. The owner’s parity thesis is priced as a scenario on the CoreWeave page ($177); the Rubin-per-GPU bear is a scenario on all three, and it bites hardest on the IREN and Nebius ladders. Open for a ruling: running costs in dollars per MW on all three pages, and IREN’s unsigned book as a tenor mix.playbook §6e · ramp-core renew
